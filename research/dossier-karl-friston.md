## PART I — BACKGROUND: THE CORE FRAMEWORK (pre-Aug 2025, foundational — cite as background, not "recent")

Needed to make sense of everything below.

**Free Energy Principle (FEP):** Living/self-organizing systems persist by minimizing "surprise" (technically: variational free energy, an upper bound on surprise/negative log-evidence) about their sensory states. Plain language: any system that keeps existing as a bounded, recognizable "thing" (a cell, an organism, a person) must act as if it's constantly trying to confirm its own predictions and avoid being surprised by the world — otherwise it would dissolve into its surroundings.

**Active Inference:** The corollary — organisms minimize surprise two ways: updating beliefs to match the world (perception) or acting on the world to make it match beliefs (action). Perception and action are the same underlying process (inference) pointed in different directions.

**Markov Blanket:** The statistical/informational boundary separating a system's "internal states" from "external states," mediated by sensory states (world → self) and active states (self → world). Friston's technical definition of what individuates a "thing" — a cell, a person, an institution — from everything else. Per the National Science Review interview (Friston, April 2024, background): "to be an intelligent or sentient entity, one has to possess a Markov blanket that individuates you from everything else"; and, crucially, "you can never measure directly what is inside the Markov blanket that envelops another person's brain" — you can only infer it indirectly (neuroimaging etc.). Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11060478/

**Self-Evidencing:** Friston's term for what organisms/brains fundamentally do — "the imperative for perception, cognition and action to maximize (gather) evidence for the brain's generative (world) model." The self, on this account, is not a thing but a process: an ongoing act of world-modeling that also evidences its own existence.

**REBUS model (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019, background — "REstricted BUS" / Relaxed Beliefs Under pSychedelics):** The original Friston-adjacent account of psychedelic ego dissolution. Psychedelics relax high-precision, high-level priors in the brain's hierarchical generative model (a "flattening" of the belief hierarchy), which lets normally-suppressed bottom-up signal through and destabilizes rigid beliefs — including the belief structure that constitutes the self-model. Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REBUS

**"Dissolving the Self: Active Inference, Psychedelics, and Ego-Dissolution" (George Deane, 2020, *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* — not authored by Friston but built directly on his framework, essential background):** Formalizes ego-dissolution as a collapse in the "temporal thickness" of the self-model. The self-model is understood as something built from a temporally deep generative model — one that represents extended sequences of potential future states ("what will happen to me if..."). Psychedelics lower the precision (confidence-weighting) of high-level priors via an elevated Bayesian learning rate; this causes the deep temporal model — and the self-model riding on top of it — to collapse toward the present moment, producing the subjective sense of "self" dissolving. Source: https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/view/8955

Background note on Living Mirrors #57 (James Cooke's podcast) — an earlier (2021) Friston conversation specifically on REBUS/psychedelics/consciousness — is NOT recent, but is a useful primer episode: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N4dJ13XzeZA

## PART II — RECENT MATERIAL (August 2025 – August 2026), chronological

### 1. "The paradox of the self-studying brain" — Battaglia, Servajean & Friston, *Physics of Life Reviews*, Vol. 52 (March 2025; still within trailing edge of relevance)
A paper on self-reference: the brain trying to study itself. Central claim: "a paradox arises when perception and interoception compete in the study of oneself" — i.e., the very organ doing the observing (the brain) is also the thing being observed, and the evidentiary bases for perception (of the outside world) and interoception (of the inner body/self) are not the same kind of evidence. The authors argue integrating neuroscience with neurophenomenology (first-person, structured self-report) is needed to resolve this. Directly relevant to ego dissolution: if selfhood is partly constituted by the brain's model of itself, and that self-modeling is paradoxical/unstable by nature, this gives a theoretical opening for why the self-model can destabilize (meditation, psychedelics, psychiatric illness).
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1571064524001787 ; PDF: https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/b761fad8-6f39-4279-8242-d21c9e88ec85/Battaglia%20et%20al.,%202025%20-%20PLRV.pdf

### 2. "Self-orthogonalizing attractor neural networks emerging from the free energy principle" — Spisak & Friston, arXiv (submitted May 2025, revised May 2026)
Formalizes how attractor networks (stable "basins" the brain's dynamics settle into) emerge naturally from the FEP, with self-organizing inference/learning dynamics. Technical/mathematical — relevant as the substrate-level account of how a stable "self-pattern" could emerge and re-stabilize (or be knocked out of one attractor into another — relevant metaphor for ego dissolution as attractor-switching). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22749

### 3. "How do inner screens enable imaginative experience? Applying the free-energy principle directly to the study of conscious experience" — Fields, Albarracin, Friston, Kiefer, Ramstead, Safron, *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, 2025
Proposes "inner screens" — internal informational boundaries (a variant/extension of Markov blanket thinking) — on which imaginative content (memory, planning, visualization) is encoded separately from external perception, explaining why imagination can still surprise you even though you generated it yourself. Key self-relevant claim: the architecture "cleanly separates the experience of [a whole agent] S from the potential experiences of any of its components" — i.e., a system (like you) can be a unified experiencer even though built from many sub-systems that might have their own local "experience." Also: the "executive/metacognitive system is just an ordinary agent... with only imperfect models of its own generative processes" — the self cannot fully know how its own actions are generated. This is a formal argument for the opacity of the self to itself. Source: https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf009/8117684

### 4. "A Beautiful Loop: An Active Inference Theory of Consciousness" — Ruben Laukkonen, Karl Friston, Shamil Chandaria, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*, 2025 — **the single most directly relevant paper for the book**
Three conditions for consciousness, all cast in active-inference/FEP terms:
1. **Reality Model** — a unified, explorable epistemic field/world-model (body, environment, memory, self, all integrated).
2. **Inferential Competition / "Bayesian Binding"** — competing candidate inferences fight for precision-weighting; only ones that coherently reduce long-run uncertainty "win" and enter the Reality Model.
3. **Epistemic Depth** — the recursive, self-referential loop: the Reality Model's own architecture shares information about itself with itself, so that "the [model] contains the inference that it exists." This is the titular "beautiful loop" — a strange loop of self-reference, reminiscent of Hofstadter.

Verbatim quote: *"The Reality Model is being shared with its own underlying architecture, constantly confirming to itself its own existence — i.e., 'knowing' itself."* The authors call this recursive self-confirmation "toroidal epistemicity."

**Direct implications for ego dissolution / meditation / psychedelics (this is the payload for your book):**
- **Meditation:** "epistemic depth is maximally high, while the reality model is maximally simple" — i.e., advanced meditation states are described as *more* self-referential awareness but with *less* content in the model (a stripped-down world-model that is intensely aware of itself). Deep cessation states ("Bayesian unbinding") are framed as a temporary collapse of the coherent Reality Model — mapped explicitly onto the Buddhist concept of śūnyatā (emptiness). Co-author Laukkonen (a meditation researcher) states: *"The reality model is just a reality model. It's constantly being reconstructed and deconstructed and is malleable."* And on method: *"You can't possibly be a meditator and then work on consciousness without being informed by your own practice."*
- **Psychedelics:** *"psychedelics reliably increase epistemic depth"* — producing the classic sense of expanded/heightened consciousness — but this is explicitly decoupled from accuracy: more recursive self-awareness ≠ more true beliefs. They introduce **FIBUS** ("False Insights and Beliefs Under Psychedelics") as a caution alongside REBUS: *"A little bit more flexibility for most humans is a good thing... sometimes that can go terribly wrong."* Laukkonen advocates "epistemic humility" over treating psychedelic insights as automatically "valuable and true."
- **What changes in advanced practitioners isn't a fixed trait** but "reactivity, the kind of grasping stickiness of models, and the willingness to change your mind in light of new information" — i.e., ego dissolution/insight work changes how tightly the self-model is held, not some separate neural trait.

Sources: ScienceDirect (paywalled abstract page, 403 to full fetch): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763425002970 ; author's own plain-language explainer (Substack, Sept 2024, pre-journal-publication): https://rubenlaukkonen.substack.com/p/a-beautiful-loop ; excellent plain-language journalistic treatment, *Asterisk Magazine*, July 2025, "Consciousness Catching Its Own Tail": https://asteriskmag.com/issues/10/consciousness-catching ; OSF preprint: https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/daf5n

### 5. IAI TV article, "Reality Is a Creation of Consciousness" — Karl Friston, published September 17, 2025

It generates predictions, hypotheses, fantasies, explanations that best explain the sensory impressions."* On the self/world boundary, the piece is explicitly subtitled around consciousness as **"the boundary between the self and the world."** On epistemic limits: *"we do not have access to the causes of our sensations"*; *"the brain is skull-bound, it lives in the dark."* On method: *"if I'm tracking the world in the right kind of way, there will be no prediction error."* Frames the FEP as unifying physics and psychology and offering "new ways to understand and treat mental illness." ### 6. "The Physics of Sentience" — invited talk, Pattern Recognition Lab, Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg, October 24, 2025
Abstract argument: for a system to differentiate itself from its environment, its internal and external states "must be conditionally independent" — this conditional independence is precisely what gives internal states an "information geometry" (a formal, describable relationship) to beliefs about the external world. Consequence: "internal states will appear to infer — and act on — their world to preserve their integrity." This is Bayesian mechanics = self-evidencing, stated as a near-physical law rather than a psychological metaphor — i.e., Friston is making the case that having-a-self-model is not optional or add-on for any bounded self-organizing system, it's what boundedness *means*. Source: https://lme.tf.fau.de/news/invited-talk-prof-dr-karl-friston-the-physics-of-sentience-may-2nd-2025-10-am-cet/

### 10. Brains Blog, "The Physics of 'As If'" — Karl Friston, published December 5, 2025 (Friston's own essay, response to critics)
Friston defends the FEP against the "it's just a metaphor / 'as if' idealization" charge using renormalization-group theory (the physics tool for describing how the "right" description of a system changes depending on the scale you look at it). His claim: neural dynamics can be described *exactly* — not merely approximately — as inference under a generative model, at the appropriate scale (analogy: astronomers can describe the moon as exactly spherical at their scale of interest, even though craters exist at finer resolution). Self-relevant quote: models — including his own theoretical apparatus — are "parts of my generative model — and possibly yours, if you are sufficiently like me" — i.e., even the theory of the self-model is itself content generated by a self-model, an explicitly self-referential, "eating its own tail" move consistent with the Beautiful Loop paper above. Wolfgang Stegemann's rebuttal (in comments) argues this is a category error: mathematical isomorphism between neural dynamics and Bayesian inference does not establish identity between them; physical properties are not semantic properties just because you can coarse-grain between them. Source: https://philosophyofbrains.com/2025/12/05/the-physics-of-as-if.aspx

## PART III — SYNTHESIS: What his framework says the self computationally IS, and what "dissolving" it means

**What the self IS (across all recent sources, consistently):**
1. Not a thing, not a location in the brain — a *process*: an ongoing, recursive act of generative modeling that includes a model of itself ("I") and its own doings ("do"), layered on top of basic homeostatic regulation.
2. Bounded by a Markov blanket — a statistical/informational boundary, not a physical wall — that individuates "you" from "everything else," mediated by sensory and active states.
3. An *attractor* — a small, bounded region of possible states consistent with staying alive/coherent (the Melbourne/London vs. 36.2–37.8°C example), not a fixed essence.
4. Recursively self-referential — per the Beautiful Loop paper, consciousness/selfhood specifically requires the model's own architecture to "know" that it exists (epistemic depth) — a strange loop, not just a rich model of the external world.
5. Necessarily opaque to itself — per the "inner screens" paper, even the metacognitive/executive system is "just an ordinary agent" with only imperfect access to how its own processes are generated. The self can never fully model itself from the inside.

**What "dissolving" the ego means, mechanistically, across sources:**
- It is fundamentally a **precision-weighting** phenomenon — turning down the confidence/gain on high-level, self-referential priors (REBUS, 2025 Precision Psychiatry abstract).
- It is a **collapse of temporal depth** — the self-model is built from deep hierarchical predictions about one's own future; flatten that hierarchy and the sense of an extended "self across time" collapses toward the present (Deane 2020, background but foundational).
- It can be a **deliberate simplification, not just a breakdown** — in meditation specifically, Friston/Laukkonen's 2025 line is that the Reality Model becomes *maximally simple* while self-referential depth (awareness-of-awareness) stays *maximally high* — i.e., meditative ego-loss and psychedelic ego-loss are not identical mechanisms even though both are "ego dissolution": psychedelics crank precision/flexibility broadly (with a risk of false insights — FIBUS), meditation specifically trains down model complexity while training up recursive self-monitoring.
- Complete cessation ("Bayesian unbinding," mapped onto Buddhist śūnyatā) is framed as a genuine, temporary collapse of the coherent Reality Model itself — the most literal "self stops modeling" event in the corpus.

**Evolution/throughline across the 12-month window:** The clearest development is convergence between Friston's own solo output (Physics of Sentience talk, Physics of "As If" essay, IAI pieces) — which stays close to the physics/Bayesian-mechanics register — and the more explicitly consciousness/meditation/psychedelic-facing collaborative work with Laukkonen and Chandaria (Beautiful Loop) and with the Cambridge precision-psychiatry program, which is where he explicitly, directly addresses meditation, dissociation, flow, and (by extension of the REBUS lineage) psychedelics as different settings of the same precision/depth mechanism. The AI-and-agency interviews (Brains Blog, Eye on AI, IWAI) run in parallel and use the *absence* of a genuine self-model (LLMs as "just a mapping between content and content") as the negative-space definition of what a self is — arguably his most quotable one-liner of the period for illustrating what a self is *not*.

**Disagreements to flag for the book:** (1) Evan Thompson/enactivism — perception happens organism-in-environment, not "in the dark, skull-bound" brain (direct rebuttal of Friston's own September 2025 framing); (2) Wolfgang Stegemann (in Brains Blog comment threads, Nov–Dec 2025) — FEP is unfalsifiable, conflates thermodynamic/informational "free energy," and smuggles in teleology/a homunculus it claims to dissolve; (3) Jan Westerhoff (Buddhist no-self scholar) positioned opposite Friston on the March 2026 IAI panel — worth a follow-up watch for direct exchange, since Westerhoff's own scholarly position (there is no unified self at all, even conventionally) is the sharpest possible foil to Friston's "self as bounded attractor" view.
